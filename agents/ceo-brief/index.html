Data notes

  • Synthetic data from a seeded Python script, for a fictional brand: daily net sales, comp, gross margin and new customers by brand and region, this year and last year (same weekdays, 364 days earlier), plus two weeks of product and customer data.
  • Freshness. Each region-day is compared with its own normal ratio to last year. Below 70% of normal and more than $25K short, it is flagged. The brief stops at the last day with no flags; a flagged day before that is a weak day, not missing data, because later days loaded in full.
  • The read is written by rules, not a model: growth vs last year first, regions by their contribution to growth, moves under half a point called "in line". It always reads month to date and follows the brand and region filters.
  • Contribution = a row's change ÷ the scope's last-year sales, so rows add up to the total. Japan also shows growth in local currency.
  • Chatbot. Answers to the four suggested questions are built in the browser from the facts of the view you have selected, then checked before they are shown. In production a language model writes the answer and the same checks run on it; this public page has no model, because it can't safely hold an API key.
  • Schedule. Calendar, email and people are synthetic. "Mark resolved" is kept in this browser; drafts are never sent.
  • Feedback review shows what the owner sees each week: ratings and reasons grouped into themes, with a suggested fix for each. The week is synthetic; your own 👍 / 👎 are added to it and stay in this browser.

The problem

Senior leaders start the day with the same questions: how did we do yesterday, is anything going wrong, what did the market do, and what do I need to decide today. The answers lived in different reports, inboxes and calendars.

They didn't need more dashboards. They needed the few things that matter, already written, and a way to ask "why?" without waiting for an analyst.

My role

I owned the analytics and insights layer — which numbers make the brief, how they're compared, and how each line is worded so an executive can read it in seconds — and the chatbot that answers follow-up questions from the same data.

I also designed the guardrails around it: the freshness check that stops the brief at the last complete day, and the checks every chatbot answer passes before it is shown.

How it works

  1. Overnight refreshYesterday's sales land in the warehouse; share prices and news are pulled in; the schedule is built from the reader's email and calendar.
  2. Freshness checkEach region-day is compared with its own normal. A region still loading stops the brief at the last complete day — and a weak but complete day is labelled as one, not hidden.
  3. The readRules, not a model, write the sales summary: what changed, then what drives it — brand, region, category, new customers — with one number and one comparison per line.
  4. Numbers to bringThe same figures follow the reader into the day: each meeting on the schedule carries the numbers it needs, and each news story says why it matters for ours.
  5. ChatbotAnswers come only from the brief's figures for the view on screen, and every figure carries a citation to where it appears on the page. Anything else gets "not in this brief".
  6. ChecksBefore an answer is shown, a checker confirms every number is cited and matches the page, direction words match the signs, and "grew the most" matches the ranking. A failing answer is retried, then withheld. Email never reaches the chat.
  7. Drafts, not sendsEvery item on the schedule comes with a reply draft. The agent writes; the reader decides and presses send.
  8. Feedback loopEvery answer can be rated, with a reason. Each week the agent groups the feedback and drafts fixes, the owner decides, and every fix becomes a regression test.

Outcome

The CEO and senior executives get their morning back. Instead of going back and forth between reports, data sources and news sites, everything they need to start the day is in one place — how the business did, what needs them this week, and what the market is saying.

Because every figure on the page and in the chat comes from the same numbers, a follow-up question gets an answer in seconds, without waiting for an analyst.
